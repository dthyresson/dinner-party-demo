### Main

**Roast Turkey — 16 lb, confirmed by DT.**

Method as supplied: 325F oven; breast-side up on a rack in a shallow roasting pan; brush the breast with vegetable oil or melted butter; tent loosely with foil once the breast is golden; done at 165F in the thickest part of the breast away from the bone and in the thighs; stand 10 minutes before slicing.

**16 lb at 325F: 3 3/4-4 1/4 hours.** That weight is no longer my assumption — DT set it. It sits in the 14-18 lb band and the time above is the recipe's for that band.

**Three gaps in this recipe, all of which matter:**
1. Its times are for an **unstuffed** bird. A stuffed cavity is denser, and the times above do not cover the batch going inside.
2. It says nothing about **how much stuffing fits** a given weight of bird.
3. It gives **no temperature for the stuffing** — the 165F is stated for breast and thigh only. A stuffed bird needs the stuffing checked in its own right.

**Honey Spiral Sliced Bone-In Ham, half — second main.**

About 10 lb, bone-in, spiral sliced. Directions as given: bring up to room temperature and serve. No cooking time or oven temperature, because it needs none — it's served as it comes once it has lost its chill.

It is the one dish that costs no oven time. It doesn't create capacity the others were short of; it just doesn't add demand.

**Timing is the thing to get right.** "Room temperature" carries no duration, and it depends on the room and the ham's starting temperature. Served cold in the middle, the point of it is lost, so it wants to come out early and be checked rather than scheduled.

Servings unstated. About 10 lb beside a 16 lb bird is a substantial second main, and with 14 at the table that's the right shape — the ham takes the pressure off the bird.

**No glaze is added at the venue — DT's decision.** That removes a mixed-in-the-kitchen step, so nothing brushed on can carry a nut or poppy. It does not make the ham uncoated: a Carando honey spiral carries its honey coating from the plant, so the pack still gets its label read, there's just one source of coating instead of two.

## Safety notes

- **Stuffing inside the bird is the risk on this menu.** A packed cavity insulates: the meat and the stuffing cook at different rates, and the bird being done does not mean the stuffing is. The supplied recipe does not cover this, so until a stuffing temperature is supplied I am not treating the in-bird batch as safe. Raw bird juices also cross-contact every surface they touch at a venue I have not seen.
- **Marshmallows normally contain gelatin**, so the sweet potato casserole isn't vegetarian unless the pack says otherwise. It has not been tagged vegetarian.
- **Mustard is a declared allergen** in several jurisdictions, and honey mustard can carry egg depending on brand. Two jars, two labels.
- **The ham is pork.**
- **Shellfish**: no dish on this list contains shellfish, so the whole-menu elimination my dietary rules demand is met on ingredients. Not a certified allergen-free claim — labels, stock and cross-contact are unverified.
- **Gluten**: green bean casserole (soy sauce, fried onions), stuffing (bread crumbs), corn pudding (flour), pumpkin pie (shell). The ham's glaze is unverified.
- **Dairy**: green bean casserole, stuffing, corn pudding, carrots, sweet potato casserole, mashed potatoes, pumpkin pie. Turkey is dairy-free only if oil is used.
- **Egg**: stuffing, corn pudding; possibly the honey mustard.
- **Soy**: green bean casserole.
- **Tree nuts and poppy seeds — one guest, confirmed.** No dish on this menu lists either as an ingredient. That is not the same as safe, and the difference matters here. The exposure is in packaged lines and cross-contact statements, not in my recipes:
  - **Poppy seed**: the real risk is bread crumbs in the stuffing — plain crumbs, not seeded or multi-grain, and the label read. Pie shell and fried onions are the other two labels to check.
  - **Tree nuts**: vanilla ice cream and marshmallows commonly carry "may contain tree nuts" statements from shared production lines; the ham's glaze and the fried onions want reading too.
  - Actions I'd hold the kitchen to: plain bread crumbs only, an ice cream with no nut statement, every label read, and the pie sliced and served before the mustards and any seeded product are on the table.
- **Severity: severe, and no cross-contamination — confirmed by DT.** That disqualifies any packaged line carrying a "may contain tree nuts", "produced in a facility" or equivalent statement; at this severity those are refusals, not cautions. Highest risk in order: vanilla ice cream (the pie is built on it), marshmallows, crispy fried onions, bread crumbs, pie shell, the ham's glaze. Any line that can't be verified comes off the menu or off that guest's plate. Kitchen controls required: boards, knives and cloths that have never touched nuts; oven racks and hob wiped down; hands washed between tasks; dishes covered; no shared jars at the table — mustards spooned out, never dipped. **I cannot certify this at a borrowed kitchen whose history I don't know, and I won't say the dinner is safe for that guest.** The decision about what they eat belongs with them and their household.
- Cross-contact and packaged-label checks still to verify: all of them, at the venue.
