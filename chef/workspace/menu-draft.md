### Main

**Roast Turkey — recipe held, from the host.**

Method as supplied: 325F oven; breast-side up on a rack in a shallow roasting pan; brush the breast with vegetable oil or melted butter; tent loosely with foil once the breast is golden; done at 165F in the thickest part of the breast away from the bone and in the thighs; stand 10 minutes before slicing.

Times by weight at 325F: 6-8 lb 2 1/4-2 3/4 h · 8-12 lb 2 3/4-3 h · 12-14 lb 3-3 3/4 h · 14-18 lb 3 3/4-4 1/4 h · 18-20 lb 4 1/4-4 1/2 h · 20-24 lb 4 1/2-5 h · 24-28 lb 5-5 1/2 h.

**Sizing, and whose number it is.** The recipe gives times by weight but no weight for a headcount, so the bird's size is my judgment, not the source's. DT's instruction is to size for 12 with leftovers. My proposal: **16 lb**, inside the 14-18 lb band, 3 3/4-4 1/4 hours. It is the one number in this menu I chose rather than read. Say the word and it changes; the time moves with it.

**Three gaps in this recipe, all of which matter:**
1. Its times are for an **unstuffed** bird. A stuffed cavity is denser, and the times above do not cover the batch going inside.
2. It says nothing about **how much stuffing fits** a given weight of bird.
3. It gives **no temperature for the stuffing** — the 165F is stated for breast and thigh only. A stuffed bird needs the stuffing checked in its own right.

**Honey Spiral Sliced Bone-In Ham, half — second main, added at DT's direction.**

About 10 lb, bone-in, spiral sliced. Directions as given: bring up to room temperature and serve. No cooking time or oven temperature is supplied because it doesn't need one — this kind of ham is served as it comes once it has lost its chill.

It is the one dish on this menu that costs no oven time. That does not create capacity the turkey and sides were short of; it simply doesn't add to the demand.

**Timing is the thing to get right.** How long it takes to come up to room temperature depends on the room and on the ham's starting temperature, and I have no number for either. Served cold in the middle, the point of it is lost; it should go out of the fridge early enough, and that is a call for whoever is in that kitchen.

Servings are unstated. About 10 lb of bone-in ham is a substantial second main beside a 16 lb bird, and I won't invent a portion count for it.

## Safety notes

- **Stuffing inside the bird is the risk on this menu.** A packed cavity insulates: the meat and the stuffing cook at different rates, and the bird being done does not mean the stuffing is. The supplied recipe does not cover this, so until a stuffing temperature is supplied I am not treating the in-bird batch as safe. Raw bird juices also cross-contact every surface they touch at a venue I have not seen.
- **Marshmallows normally contain gelatin.** The sweet potato casserole is therefore not vegetarian unless the pack says otherwise, and I have not tagged it vegetarian. If the menu must satisfy a vegetarian, either source a gelatin-free marshmallow or serve that dish without the topping.
- **Mustard is a declared allergen** in several jurisdictions, and honey mustard can carry egg depending on the brand. Two mustards means two labels to read.
- **The ham is pork.** Anyone avoiding pork still has the turkey — which is part of why two mains is easier than one.
- **Shellfish**: no dish on this list contains shellfish, so the whole-menu elimination my dietary rules demand is met on ingredients. Not a certified allergen-free claim — labels, stock and cross-contact are unverified.
- **Gluten**: green bean casserole (soy sauce, fried onions), stuffing (bread crumbs), corn pudding (flour), pumpkin pie (shell). Four dishes. The ham's glaze is unverified.
- **Dairy**: green bean casserole (soup, milk), stuffing (butter), corn pudding, carrots (butter), sweet potato casserole (butter), pumpkin pie. Turkey is dairy-free only if oil is used, not butter.
- **Egg**: stuffing, corn pudding; possibly the honey mustard.
- **Soy**: green bean casserole.
- **Nuts**: none listed in any dish.
- **Clean of all five by ingredient**: cranberry orange relish, pearl onions, turkey, sweet potatoes themselves — though the liqueur's gluten status is unstated and a brined or basted bird would change the turkey line.
- Cross-contact and packaged-label checks still to verify: all of them, at the venue.

## Still blocking

1. A temperature for the stuffing in the cavity, and the cavity capacity for a 16 lb bird.
2. Method, oven temperature, time and pan for Mimi's stuffing — without them the side batch is not cookable either.
3. Allergies and dietary needs for the 12 — the allergen map cannot be applied without them, and the gelatin and mustard questions ride on it.
4. Hob rings, freezer space, and baking dishes large enough for 1.5x sweet potato casserole.
5. Whether the bird is fresh or frozen. If frozen, thawing for a 16 lb bird starts days before, not on the day.
6. When the ham comes out of the fridge — "bring up to room temperature" carries no duration, and a ham still cold in the middle at 2 PM defeats the dish.
